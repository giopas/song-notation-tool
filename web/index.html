🎸 Song Notation Tool

How this works

  • A song is a list of sections (Intro, Verse, Chorus…), each with a name, an instrument, a repeat count, and one chart line.
  • The chart line is typed, not clicked: chord/note symbols in order, separated by spaces — e.g. B F# G E. Add a fret number right after a symbol with no space to show it too — e.g. 5A means "fret 5 on note A".
  • Repeats: put (x3) after a section's repeat field, or wrap part of a line in brackets with a repeat — [5A 7D]x2 plays that pair twice before continuing.
  • Riffs: a block referenced by name in more than one section — edit it once, every section using it updates. (Manage riffs from the desktop app for now.)
  • Transpose shifts every chord/fret in a section (or the whole song) by semitones, without rewriting what you typed — safe to undo by transposing back.
  • Rest: type rest in a chart line to mark a measure where you don't play — shows as "rest" in the chart and export.
  • Tab grid: switch a section's render mode to "Tab" or "Both" (dropdown in the section header) to show an editable tablature grid. Add measures with the "+ Measure" button and type fret numbers into the cells.
  • Licks: write a short tab figure inline with {G 5 7 5 | D - - 3} — one line per string, printed in among the chords at the point it's played.
  • Free text: switch a section's render mode to "Free" for a plain text box instead of a chart line — anything you type there is kept and exported verbatim, for the bits this notation doesn't cover.
  • The buttons beside the chart line drop notation at the cursor — rests, repeat barlines, endings, groups, annotations — so you don't have to remember the syntax.
  • Lyrics holds reference text for a section or the whole song — paste it, import a .txt file, or open a browser tab to search for it online. It's never parsed, aligned, or exported automatically; just there to work against by eye.
  • A parse error in a chart line never clears what you typed — it's shown inline in red under the field, and the last valid render stays visible above it.

    Licks — a bit of tab, in among the chords
    
        
    Some things aren't a chord. When you want to remember the actual
          notes of a short figure, write it in braces at the point it's
          played:
    
        
    C {G 5 7 5 | D - - 3} G Am
    
        
    Each |-separated line is one string — its name, then its
          frets. Positions line up by column across the lines, so a fret on
          G at the third position sounds with whatever is third on
          D; - means that string isn't played there, and
          x means muted. Write as many lines as the figure needs.
    
        
    It prints as a small tab block sitting under its own column in the
          chart row, so it stays where it belongs in the sequence rather than
          being exiled to a separate grid. A G: 5 7 5 spelling works
          too if the colon reads better to you. Transposing a section moves a
          lick's frets with it — same strings, shifted positions.
    

        
    Marks
    
        
    These are the standard sheet-music
          symbols, written out as plain text you can type. They exist so a repeated part
          is written once and pointed at, instead of copied out again — which is what
          keeps a song on one page.
    
        
    
          
    TypeNameWhat it tells the player
    
          
            
    |:Repeat-open
                Start of a repeated stretch. Opens the section that :| closes.
    
            
    :|Repeat-close
                End of a repeated stretch — go back to the last |: (or to the
                    start of the song if there isn't one) and play it through once more.
    
            
    |1.1st ending
                Play this the first time through the repeat, then go back.
                    Everything after the mark belongs to the ending, up to the next one.
    
            
    |2.2nd ending
                Skip the 1st ending on the way through the second time and play this
                    instead, then carry on. The usual "two different last bars" case.
    
            
    %Simile
                Play this bar exactly like the previous one. Saves rewriting a
                    groove that doesn't change.
    
            
    restRest / tacet
                Don't play here — silence for this measure. Worth writing down: an
                    intentional gap and a forgotten bar look the same otherwise.
    
            
    segnoSegno (𝄋)
                A named place-marker. On its own it does nothing; it's the point
                    ds sends you back to. Italian for "sign".
    
            
    codaCoda (𝄌)
                Marks the tail end — the separate final passage you jump to after
                    the last repeat, instead of playing the section out as written.
                    Italian for "tail".
    
            
    dcD.C. — da capo
                "From the top": go back to the very beginning of the song and play
                    through again from there.
    
            
    dsD.S. — dal segno
                "From the sign": go back to the segno mark — not to the
                    start — and play on from there. Use it when the repeat begins
                    partway in, after an intro you don't play twice.
    
              
        
    

        
    The common shape: a song ends
          ds (or dc), you play back through from the
          segno, and on that second pass you break off at
          coda for the real ending. Written out in full that's three
          near-identical pages; with these marks it's three words.
    

        
    None of these change the notes — they only change the
          order you play them in. The buttons beside each chart line insert
          any of them at the cursor, so you never have to remember the exact spelling.
    

        
    Render modes
    
        
    Chart — chord/note symbols in a row (the chart line).
    
           Tab — editable tablature grid (fret numbers on strings).
    
           Both — chart line above, tab grid below.
    
           Free — a plain multi-line text box instead. Nothing in it is
           parsed, transposed, or validated; it's printed in the TXT/PDF export exactly
           as typed. Use it for a section the notation above doesn't cover.
    
        
    Switch with the dropdown in each section's header. The tab grid
           has its own "+ Measure" button and beats-per-measure selector.
