Song Notation Tool

How this works

  • A song is a list of sections (Intro, Verse, Chorus…). Each has a name, an instrument and one chart line. + Part adds another instrument's line, as a chart line, a tab grid, both, or free text.
  • You type the chart line: chord or note symbols in order, separated by spaces, such as B F# G E. Put a fret number right before a symbol, with no space, to show it too: 5A means fret 5 on A.
  • Repeats go in the chart line: put a run in brackets with a repeat. [5A 7D]x2 plays the pair twice, then carries on, and [G D Em C]x2 plays the whole line twice.
  • Riffs: a block used by name in more than one section. Edit it once and every section that uses it changes. Riffs at the top lists them; select part of a chart line and click → riff to make one from it. While you type, a short list suggests the song's riffs, its sections after = and its named licks after {.
  • Transpose shifts every chord and fret in a section, or the whole song, by semitones without rewriting what you typed. Transposing back undoes it.
  • Rests: type rest in a chart line for a measure you do not play. It shows as "rest" in the chart and the export.
  • Tab grid: click Tab or Both in the section header to get a tablature grid you can edit. Add measures with + Measure and type fret numbers into the cells. Out of room in a measure? +2 and +4 over it add empty beats to every string of that measure, and − takes empty ones off the end.
  • Long sections: put // where the chart should start a new line. Each line lines up its own columns. //> starts a new line and indents it, so a phrase can sit inside the one above.
  • Licks: write a short tab figure inline with {G 5 7 5 | D - - 3}, one part per string. It prints among the chords, where it is played.
  • Techniques on a lick or tab cell: 5h7 hammer-on, 7p5 pull-off, 5/7 and 7\5 slides, 7b9 bend, 7b9r7 bend and release, 7~ vibrato, x muted.
  • Dynamics and accents: p, mf, ff… where the change happens, 5A^ for an accent, and runs over several notes: [pm 5A 5A 5A] palm mute, [cresc A D E], [dim E D A].
  • Parts: + Part under a section adds another instrument's line, such as the guitar chords under the bass. Layout → Instruments picks which instruments print.
  • Named licks: give a lick a name, {Riff1 = G 5 7 5 | D - - 3}, and play it again anywhere in the song as {Riff1}, with a repeat if you like: {Riff1}x3. Edit the lick once and every place that plays it follows, as with a section reference.
  • Lyrics: paste the words, import a .txt file, or search for them online (nothing is fetched for you). Mark the sheet with === Verse 1 === lines by dragging the section chips in the Lyrics dialog, and Split by markers gives each block to its section. Markers never print, and the words are never lined up with the chart. Whether and where they print (all together at the start, at the end or down a left column, with each section, or not at all) is set in Layout, bottom left.
  • Layout (bottom left) holds every print setting: size, columns, colour, section names, lyrics, licks and chord shapes.
  • Chords keeps a list of voicings (x32010 is C) and prints them as diagrams at the start or the end of the chart.
  • Free text: set a section's render mode to Free for a plain text box instead of a chart line. What you type there is kept and exported exactly as typed, for whatever this notation does not cover.
  • The buttons under the chart line you are typing in put notation in at the cursor (rests, repeat barlines, endings, groups, annotations), so you do not have to remember how to write it.
  • A mistake never clears what you typed. It shows in red under the field, and the last chart that worked stays visible, dimmed, until the line is fixed.
  • Escape closes any dialog.
  • Every PDF export carries its song. Import song… (left, under the song list) opens such a PDF, or a .sng file, as a new song.
Songs

    Songs folder
    Changing and moving the folder works in the app's own window. In a browser tab, start the server with --dir to use a different folder.
    Layout: how this song prints
    Page

    Fit to page enlarges the type to fill the paper. Fit to one page shrinks it as far as needed to get the whole song on one sheet. Floor prints the largest type that fits on one sheet, for a page on the floor at your feet: it picks the columns and the orientation itself, keeps the title and section names small, and prints tab at half size.

    Two columns halve the height a chart needs, and the fit uses the space for bigger type. Auto only splits the page when that helps.

    Colour tells section types apart at a glance. The notes stay black either way.

    Sections

    A band above each section, or a column down its left. The column fits far more on a page.

    Lyrics

    Licks & chords

    {Riff1} printed as its tab again, or only its name and count: "Riff1 (x3)".

    Edit the shapes with Chords in the toolbar.

    Instruments

    Which instruments print, when sections or parts use more than one. Untick one to print a chart for the others only.

    Chord shapes
    
      
    
        One fret per string, lowest string first, as a chord chart writes it:
        x32010 is C, 320003 is G. Frets above 9 need
        spaces: x 0 12 12 12 x. Use x for a string you do not
        play. The shapes print once, as a block of diagrams, where
        Layout → Chord shapes (bottom left) says.
      
    
      
    
      
    
      
    
      
    
          
          
          
      
    
      
    
      
    
        
      
    
    
    
  
    

  
  
    
    
    
      
    Split lyrics into sections
    
      
    
        One row per section, one block per row. The same block can go to as many
        sections as sing it, so a chorus written once can be played three times.
      
    
      
    
      
    Whether and where the words print is set in
        Layout, bottom left.
    
      
    
        
        
      
    
      
    The whole-song sheet is kept as it is, so a section you add
        later can still be given one of its blocks. The sheet itself stops printing,
        so the same words do not appear on the chart twice.

    Breaking a section over several lines
    
        
    A long section reads better split into the phrases you would write by
          hand. // anywhere on the chart line starts a new line
          there:
    
        
    5A 5D // 8F 5D 7E // 5A 5A 8F
    
        
    prints as
    
        
      5  5
  A  D
  8  5  7
  F  D  E
  5  5  8
  A  A  F
    
        
    Each line lines up its own columns, so one grid does not stretch across
          the whole section. The break only affects layout: it plays nothing, and
          the chart line itself stays one line you can edit.
    
        
    Add > to indent the line, //> for one
          step and //>> for two, so a phrase can sit inside the
          one above it:
    
        
    5A 5D //> 8F 5D 7E // 5A 5A 8F
    
        
      5  5
  A  D
        8  5  7
        F  D  E
  5  5  8
  A  A  F
    

        
    Licks: a bit of tab among the chords
    
        
    Some things are not chords. To remember the actual notes of a short
          figure, write it in braces where it is played:
    
        
    C {G 5 7 5 | D - - 3} G Am
    
        
    Each part between | is one string: its name, then its
          frets. Positions line up by column across the strings, so a fret third on
          G sounds with whatever is third on D.
          - means the string is not played there, and x
          means muted. Write as many strings as the figure needs.
    
        
    It prints as tab, one row per string with the string name in a box on
          the left, under its own column in the chart row. That keeps it in its
          place in the sequence instead of in a separate grid:
    
        
    |G|-5-7-5-|
|D|-----3-|
    
        
    On screen and in a colour PDF a lick is blue, so it is not mistaken for
          a chord symbol at a glance. The { tab } button beside the
          chart line inserts an empty one (every string of the section's
          instrument, six positions wide), so you type frets over dashes instead of
          deleting an example first. G: 5 7 5, with a colon, works too
          if you find it easier to read. Transposing a section moves a lick's frets
          with it: same strings, shifted positions.
    

        
    Playing techniques
    
        
    A lick position or a tab-grid cell can carry the usual tab letters after
          the fret: 5h7 hammer-on, 7p5 pull-off,
          5/7 slide up, 7\5 slide down, 7b9
          bend up to the pitch of fret 9, 7b9r7 bend and release,
          7~ vibrato, x muted, 5^ accented.
    
        
    |G|-5h7-7p5-|
|D|-----7b9-|
    
        
    They print as typed, and the columns widen so the strings still line up.
          Transposing moves every fret in a position.
    

        
    Dynamics, accents and runs
    
        
    Write how loud to play where it changes, as a word of its own:
          pp p mp mf f ff, printed in bold. An accent is ^
          right after a note, 5A^, and prints as > over it.
          Something that covers several notes is a run, its word first inside
          brackets:
    
        
    p [pm 5A 5A 5A 5A] mf 7D^ 7D
    
        
         P.M.-----|      >
     5  5  5  5      7  7
  p  A  A  A  A  mf  D  D
    
        
    [cresc A D E] and [dim E D A] print as hairpins in
          the PDF. A run repeats like any group: [pm 5A 5A]x2.
    

        
    Marks
    
        
    These are the usual sheet-music
          symbols, written as plain text you can type. They let you write a repeated
          part once and point back to it instead of copying it out again, which is how
          a song stays on one page.
    
        
    
          
    TypeNameWhat it tells the player
    
          
            
    |:Repeat-open
                Start of a repeated stretch. Opens the section that :| closes.
    
            
    :|Repeat-close
                End of a repeated stretch: go back to the last |: (or to the
                    start of the song if there is none) and play it through once more.
    
            
    |1.1st ending
                Play this the first time through the repeat, then go back. Prints as a
                    bracket marked "1." over the bars up to the :| or the next
                    ending.
    
            
    |2.2nd ending
                The second time through, skip the 1st ending and play this instead,
                    then carry on. Use it when the last bars differ between the passes. Its
                    bracket runs to the next ending or the end of the section.
    
            
    //Line break
                Layout only: start a new line here. Plays nothing.
    
            
    //>Line break, indented
                As above, with the new line pushed in one step per >.
    
            
    %Simile
                Play this bar exactly like the previous one, so a groove that does not
                    change is written only once.
    
            
    restRest / tacet
                Do not play here: silence for this measure. Worth writing down, because
                    otherwise a planned gap and a forgotten bar look the same.
    
            
    segnoSegno (𝄋)
                A place marker. On its own it does nothing; it is the point
                    ds sends you back to. Italian for "sign".
    
            
    codaCoda (𝄌)
                Marks the ending: the separate final passage you jump to after
                    the last repeat, instead of playing the section out as written.
                    Prints as a boxed coda sign. Italian for "tail".
    
            
    dcD.C. (da capo)
                "From the top": go back to the very beginning of the song and play
                    through again from there.
    
            
    dsD.S. (dal segno)
                "From the sign": go back to the segno mark, not to the
                    start, and play on from there. Use it when the repeat begins
                    partway in, after an intro you do not play twice.
    
              
        
    

        
    The usual shape: a song ends with ds (or
          dc), you play back through from the segno, and on
          that second pass you break off at coda for the real ending.
          Written out in full that is three almost identical pages; with these marks it
          is three words.
    

        
    None of these change the notes, only the order you play
          them in. The buttons beside each chart line insert any of them at the cursor,
          so you do not have to remember the spelling.
    

        
    Render modes
    
        
    Chart: chord and note symbols in a row (the chart line).
    
           Tab: a tablature grid you can edit (fret numbers on strings).
    
           Both: the chart line above, the tab grid below.
    
           Free: a plain text box instead. Nothing in it is parsed, transposed or
           checked, and the TXT and PDF exports print it exactly as typed. Use it for a
           section the notation above does not cover.
    
        
    Switch with Chart, Tab, Both and Free in each section's header. The tab grid
           has its own + Measure button, a length for new measures, and +2, +4 and − over each measure to make it longer or shorter.
    
      
    

      
    
        
      
    
    
    
  
    
  
  
    
    
    
      
    Riffs
    
      
    A riff is a run of chart line with a
        name. Type the name on any chart line to play it there (Riff1 x3),
        and edit it here once to change every place that plays it. To make one from a
        line you already typed, select that part and click → riff
        beside it.
